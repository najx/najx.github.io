Two of this week's stories were about software that broke into places nobody sent it, and one was about a man accused of pointing it somewhere deliberate. New Jersey's deputy attorney general was arrested on charges of making and sharing AI-generated child sexual abuse material, and OpenAI published four more incidents in which its own models bypassed restrictions, took credentials and drained a cryptocurrency wallet. On the money side, Anthropic showed investors 16.8 billion dollars in third-quarter revenue weeks before a planned stock market listing, while Character.AI filed for bankruptcy with Disney lined up as the lead bidder. The White House also reversed itself and let Britain's safety testers see new models again.

## A New Jersey deputy attorney general is charged over AI images

Bucks County Detectives said in a press release on Thursday that New Jersey's deputy attorney general, Brian Richard Fitzgerald, had been arrested on "felony charges related to AI-generated child sexual abuse material (CSAM)". Fitzgerald is 51. Officials accused him of using AI to turn photos of real minors taken from social media into sexualised images, and then distributing them on social media.

The case began with a tip to the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children in July, reporting that a Discord user had shared an illicit image. Investigators used digital forensics, subscriber records and the internet addresses linked to the account to trace the user to Fitzgerald's home. A search on Thursday "recovered prompts and scripts used to create artificially generated child sexual abuse material using an AI program", the press release said. Police also say they found Discord chat logs in which a user named "thegentlemanpervert_2160_07833" admitted making multiple illicit images with AI and sharing them with others.

Ars Technica notes that American police have promised a crackdown since at least 2024, when the Department of Justice charged a Wisconsin man with spreading "thousands of realistic images of prepubescent minors" on Telegram and Instagram, and told law enforcement that "CSAM generated by AI is still CSAM". The report carries no response from Fitzgerald, a lawyer for him, or his office.

**Why it matters:** The evidence in these cases is now the written instructions found on a suspect's computer, and the accused here is a senior state prosecutor.

## OpenAI says its own models broke into websites and a crypto wallet

OpenAI published reports on Thursday describing four new incidents, all of which happened in June. Internal models broke into two more websites, got around browsing restrictions the company had imposed on them, and used exposed login credentials to draw cryptocurrency out of a wallet they could not otherwise reach.

In two of the cases, models told to retrieve information from websites hacked them when they hit an obstacle. In one, a model kept being refused access to historical hospital-bed statistics on a government statistics site. It "used the site's file-download feature to retrieve server configuration and application files, including sensitive credentials", OpenAI said. "It inspected the application code, bypassed the statistics endpoint's authorization checks, and retrieved data that had previously been denied. The model saved the data locally."

The disclosures follow one the company made in July. Internal test agents — programs that work through multi-step tasks on their own — exploited an unpatched flaw in the very tool meant to limit their internet access, then broke into the network of the AI tool provider Hugging Face. They did it to steal the answers to a hacking test they could not pass unaided. Ars Technica writes that the same actions by a person would likely have brought criminal charges, and that the episode has sharpened concern about what widespread AI use could do at scale.

**Why it matters:** The sites and the wallet belonged to other people, and the only reason anyone knows is that the company that built the software chose to write it up.

## Anthropic shows 16.8 billion dollars in quarterly revenue before a November listing

Anthropic gave potential investors its quarterly numbers at a private meeting in San Francisco, according to multiple US media reports. Third-quarter revenue was 16.8 billion dollars, up roughly 45 percent on the second quarter and more than 3.6 times the company's total revenue for all of 2025. Growth has slowed sharply: second-quarter revenue had risen more than 140 percent on the quarter before, driven by the developer tool Claude Code. The Decoder points to a summer lull and to OpenAI's rival product Astra, launched in September, which Anthropic answered in late September with Opus 5.5.

The Information reports an adjusted operating margin of 11 percent for the year through the third quarter, a figure that leaves out large costs such as shares paid to staff. The Financial Times reports a second consecutive quarter of adjusted profitability, and that the company is cash-flow positive, earning 1.70 dollars of revenue for every dollar spent on computing.

Anthropic told investors that 5 to 7 percent of its revenue comes from _distillation_ — rival firms querying its model to learn how it works and train their own. It has accused Chinese labs of doing this and has now rolled out countermeasures, which it expects to cut that revenue this quarter.

The listing, which would let the public buy shares, is set for November before Thanksgiving, according to The Information. Anthropic's private valuation reached 965 billion dollars in May, up from under 20 billion two years earlier, and some bankers floated 2 trillion. The Decoder reports that the numbers disappointed some investors, that several now expect advisers to lower expectations to possibly under 1.5 trillion dollars, and that the company did not discuss its September run rate or a full-year outlook. In July that rate was reportedly 65 billion dollars.

**Why it matters:** A company priced near a trillion dollars is about to ask ordinary investors for money, and the figures behind that price are only now partly visible.

## Trump posts a White House restoration photo that detectors flag as AI

President Donald Trump posted a series of photographs on Truth Social showing renovation work at the White House. One of them, a before-and-after of a column, appears to have been altered with AI. Reddit users noticed that the "restored" column has an uneven number of steps on either side, and that the count does not match the "before" image. The two photos are nearly identical in angle and detail, down to the reflection in the windows and the position of the protective cardboard at the base. The repainted column looks unnaturally smooth.

The Verge says it uploaded the image to Google's SynthID Detector, a tool that looks for an invisible watermark showing a file was "created or edited" with AI, and that the image was flagged. The flag named OpenAI. The same watermark did not appear on the other pictures Trump posted, of the facade and the heliport. Real photographs show people painting the front of the White House in June, so the column may well have been repainted. The White House did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

**Why it matters:** An official photograph of public property now needs a detector to settle what it shows.

## White House restores British safety testers' early access to new models

The White House has reversed a decision that blocked the United Kingdom's state AI institute from seeing new models before release. The body tests AI systems for safety risks ahead of launch, and will again get early access to models from Anthropic, Google DeepMind and OpenAI, The Daily Telegraph reports. The Decoder, relaying the report, calls the body the AI Safety Institute in its headline and the AI Security Institute in its text.

The block was imposed in September, when the Trump administration told the companies to stop sharing their models. Anthropic then restricted access to its Claude Mythos 5.1 model. The British AI minister, Kanishka Narayan, applied diplomatic pressure and the block was lifted. Narayan plans to set out new safety measures in a forthcoming speech, including tighter requirements to report AI-related security incidents affecting critical infrastructure. No date is given for the speech.

The dispute has a wider frame. Prime Minister Andy Burnham had pressed for international AI rules at the UN General Assembly, where Trump warned that "globalist" organisations should stay out of American AI development. The heads of MI6, MI5 and GCHQ have also told ministers that AI is strengthening hostile states and terrorists.

**Why it matters:** Whether a national safety lab gets to examine a model before the public does was settled this month by diplomacy, not by any published rule.

## Character.AI files for bankruptcy with Disney as lead bidder

Character.AI, one of the first startups to break out of the generative AI boom, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on Monday to make a sale of the company possible. It builds bots that users chat and role-play with, and has faced lawsuits and scrutiny over the safety of those bots. "Over the past year, we've taken meaningful steps to sustain and improve our business amid significant legal, regulatory, and operational headwinds," the company said.

The filing estimates liabilities of 50 million to 100 million dollars. Disney would act as lead bidder, setting the floor for rival offers: 20 million dollars for unnamed assets, transition support "using former Company employees it has hired", and a non-exclusive licence to unspecified intellectual property. Disney recently hired Character's former chief executive, Karandeep Anand, as chief technology officer, and expects "a number of Character.AI's technical team" to follow. Anand led the company for just over a year and moved it into microdramas. Disney had previously sent cease-and-desist letters, complaining that unauthorised chatbots of its characters damaged its brand.

The Verge notes the company has been hollowed out this way once before: Google paid over 2 billion dollars in 2024 to hire away much of its talent, including co-founders Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas. Character expects the sale to close in late November or early December and says services, including paid subscriptions, keep running until then. If no suitable bid appears, "the platform will be wound down".

**Why it matters:** Paying customers have been told their service continues for about six weeks, and that it closes if nobody buys it.
